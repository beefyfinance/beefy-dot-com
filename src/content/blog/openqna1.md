On 22 May 2026, we hosted Beefy's first Open Q&A event on the [Beefy Discord Server](https://beefy.finance/discord). The event was a chance for Beefy's users and cowmoonity to talk openly with the contributor team, discuss their questions and ideas, and give feedback on Beefy's performance and plans.

Users were provided with a polling tool to submit their questions and conversation topics, which are reproduced below alongside their verbatim answers. A recording of the session has also been made available on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WS8o0DtOmMI).

Keen to take part in the next session? Or have questions you want answered? Head to the #-general channel on the [Beefy Discord Server](https://beefy.finance/discord) to find out about future events.

## Team

## BIFI Token

### 2. [BIFI MOO] Should the DAO become the largest holder of BIFI? Could big acquisitions create a flywheel of upward pressure on the BIFI price?

**Jack:**

> We're definitely one of the largest holders of BIFI already. The amount oscillates constantly because quite a bit of it is deployed as liquidity. But I think last time we checked, it was around 6,000 to 7,000 BIFI. So getting close to 10% of supply being held in the treasury. Some of that is non-circulating, so not really being put to any use.
>
> And some of it is in use as liquidity or some other minor use cases that we have. But in general, we already have quite a significant amount of BIFI. And the market hasn't really reacted to that fact significantly. There's not a lot of people who do the research on circulating supply and say, OK, well, there's 80K BIFI, but actually only 73K of it is actually circulating. So the problem I have with this idea of why doesn't the DAO just keep buying more BIFI?
>
> Big acquisitions creating flywheels, creating supply pressure. I just think it relies on a lot of assumptions about how the market is going to react to those events. And the market is often so detached from the underlying economics that we could spend so much of the treasury trying to acquire BIFI and end up in a position where holders don't really realize it, but they do see that our Treasury number has gone down and wonder if their BIFI is worth less as a result. So it's a nice theory, but I think in practice it's not especially doable.

## Vision / Strategy

### 3. [A] What is Beefy’s long-term vision in the next DeFi cycle: remaining primarily a yield optimizer, or evolving into a broader access layer for onchain finance?

**Jack:**

> I think a lot of people still use this just a yield optimizer tag and mistake what we're doing now for being exactly the same as when we launched in 2020. Beefy has definitely expanded over those years. And I would say we've moved to add more vertical integrations into what we do. So the biggest example of that is our CLM products. Concentrated liquidity can be much more effective in terms of generating returns, but it requires very careful management.
>
> And we sat back and observed a lot of different liquidity management protocols and finally came to the conclusion that we could do a great job of this ourselves and it would dovetail well with our traditional yield optimizer background. And over the past couple of years, concentrated liquidity has made up, if not the majority, then definitely a very large chunk of our total revenue. So that was definitely a step away from simple yield optimization towards full stack liquidity management. And thinking about that as a vertical stack, you've got compounding incentives on the top, you've got managing liquidity in the middle layer. I think the next layer down of that would be hosting that liquidity.
>
> And that's something that Uniswap v4 really opens the door to. We haven't launched anything with Uniswap v4 as yet. But as with Uniswap v3 and concentrated liquidity, we're always sat back watching. But definitely that vertical model of expanding what we do is more of interest than something like the access layer for on-chain finance, which is a really, really broad term. It suggests that you're going to be stretched across the whole market offering everything.
>
> I think I always think of it as if you don't stand for anything in particular, you're probably going to fall for everything. So we try and really make clear what we stand for. And have a very clear product identity.

## Insurance Fund

### 11. [DREAM] Could Beefy lock part of the treasury for a built-in insurance fund, similar to the Aave Umbrella program?

**Jack:**

> They have a module as part of their program where part of the funds are directed and users are incentivized to contribute. And then the idea is those funds are kept safely in case anything happens and they can be distributed to cover losses. We saw a lot of discussion about this recently with the Kelp hack because the amount of losses far exceeded the umbrella. So everyone was like, well, what was the point in that? We would just drain the umbrella and people wouldn't get it all back.
>
> And in the end, they ended up filling that hole in other means. And that I think, leads into our view on insurance fund products. Obviously, we had BSP04 back in maybe February now or January, which was to explore introducing protocol-wide coverage together with Nexus Mutual. That was a slightly different proposal focused on working with an external partner, whereas I think what this question is getting at is Beefy basically offering its own insurance fund. And its own insurance product from our treasury.
>
> I think it's dangerous and not a great idea because our treasury is so much smaller than our total TVL. So if you agree to backstop a certain amount of loss, there's a pretty good chance that that total amount of loss could happen within the course of the next year, two years, eventually. Particularly in situations where we aren't actually the ones at fault, but there's something like a stablecoin blowing up like we saw with Stream or Resolv, and our users are left in between. And if we just drain that treasury as soon as that happens, we end up in a situation where the project can't really continue to fund itself and operate, and a few users walk away happier, but everybody else has to suffer for it. The great advantage with the Nexus proposal is that we don't underwrite that risk.
>
> We don't bear that on our balance sheet. Instead, we pay out a bit of revenue every month, and we get a consistent rolling amount of coverage, and that coverage wouldn't cover all of our TVL, but there's a good chance that it would cover most of the individual exploits that we might have some user exposure to. Meaning that we don't lose our whole financial future just because of one of those situations, but we do provide some level of recovery for the users. So to be honest, I'm all in still on BSP04. I think it's the right directional decision for us, but it's something where we need to have the time to implement it properly and the funding to implement it properly.
>
> And so for right now, it's still on pause. Otherwise for me it's sadly a no to built-in insurance fund operated by Beefy.

## Technology

### 16. [A] How is Beefy preparing for Ethereum scalability and UX improvements, such as account abstraction and future rollup upgrades?

**Jack:**

> Beefy definitely is not trying to position ourselves as a leader in every technological development. We're too small and we're trying to be really good at what we do, not spread ourselves across lots of different things. So just the two examples we've got there, scalability, things like upgrades to our roll-ups, making sure that we're proactively taking advantage of bigger block space or changes in the way that blocks are generated. We are constantly paying attention to these kinds of updates. We're needing to update the app to ensure that it operates in line with the parameters of these different chains.
>
> But we're really not trying to be well ahead of that and market ourselves as this really focused on scalability product. The reverse. We want to make sure that Beefy is a consistent experience across all kinds of different chains. And so we're spending time looking at the chains that aren't scaling as fast or that have older technology and trying to make sure that that experience is consistent. Rather than saying to everyone, you should move over to Monad and MegaETH because they've got sub-second block times and all of this amazing technology.
>
> And then leaving behind the people who are still really focused on Ethereum or Binance Smart Chain, BNB Chain. As for UX improvements and account abstraction, some of these technologies are relevant to us. We do integrate lots of different kinds of wallets, for example on the account abstraction side, and we want to make sure that we are continuing to offer access through the latest and greatest technologies. But at the same time, they have no particular connection to the business model that we operate, the revenues that we generate. So we're quite happy to let the latest shiny thing go past and instead wait for something that's really going to make the difference.
